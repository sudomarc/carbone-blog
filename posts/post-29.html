The Excalibur’s Pulse: Engineering a Supercar Partnership

"The RD630 Huracán and the Countach DT/X represent distinct chapters in a shared story of ambition, where Swiss watchmaking precision meets Italian automotive ferocity."

Carbone NotesPost 292 min read

The Roger Dubuis Lamborghini partnership, now in its ninth year, has produced a lineage of timepieces that function as mechanical manifestos. Since 2017, the collaboration has yielded the Duotor Aventador S, the RD630 Huracán, the Countach DT/X, and the RD780 flyback chronographs, each referencing a specific Lamborghini model. The partnership’s longevity suggests a shared design language rather than a fleeting licensing deal. The RD630 Huracán, for instance, employs a tonneau-shaped case and a skeletonized dial that mirrors the engine architecture of the Huracán Performante, while the Countach DT/X leans into the wedge silhouette of the 1970s original, pairing it with a vertical chronograph layout. These are not merely watches with car logos; they are objects where the constraints of haute horology and the aesthetics of supercar design are forced into conversation.

Form Follows Function, but Whose Function?

Technical execution in this partnership reveals a tension between two engineering traditions. Roger Dubuis, rooted in Geneva’s movement finishing, brings complications such as the flyback chronograph—a mechanism that allows the user to reset and restart a timing function with a single pusher. This is a practical tool for pilots and racing drivers, yet its presence on a wrist intended to evoke a V12 engine raises questions about prioritization. The RD780 flyback chronograph, for example, integrates a pusher at 2 o’clock and a reset function that feels intuitive for measuring laps, but the visual language of the Lamborghini countach—sharp angles, scissor doors—does not always translate comfortably to the curvature of a watch case. The result is a series of timepieces that acknowledge their automotive inspiration while remaining steadfastly independent in their construction.

Material choices further illustrate the partnership’s compromises. Carbon fiber, a staple of Lamborghini chassis, appears in dials and bezels, offering a lightweight, matte texture that references the brand’s racing pedigree. However, the use of carbon fiber in watchmaking is not without its challenges; the material can be prone to chipping, and its production requires specialized expertise that not every maison possesses. Roger Dubuis has navigated this by pairing carbon elements with traditional metals like titanium and gold, creating a hybrid aesthetic that attempts to satisfy both the durability demands of a supercar and the preciousness expected of a luxury watch. The Duotor Aventador S, for example, utilizes a mix of King Gold and carbon, a combination that speaks to the partnership’s desire to bridge two distinct material cultures.

Ultimately, the Roger Dubuis Lamborghini partnership endures because it offers something rare in the luxury market: a genuine dialogue between two industries that rarely intersect. The watches do not simply slap a logo onto a supercar; they reinterpret the DNA of each brand through the other’s lens. The RD630 Huracán’s skeletonized movement invites the wearer to see the “engine” inside, just as the Countach DT/X’s dial geometry references the sharp, angular lines of its namesake. This is engineering as storytelling, where every screw and every gear train is a sentence in a larger narrative about speed, power, and the passage of time. The partnership’s success lies in its ability to make the abstract concrete—turning a car’s performance metrics into a watch’s complications, and a brand’s heritage into a tangible object on the wrist.
